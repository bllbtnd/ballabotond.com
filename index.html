Where this comes from

Esztergom → Pécs → Budapest

I was born in Budapest, where the parliament towers over the river and history seeps through every cobblestone, and grew up an hour away in Esztergom. Baroque streets, the Danube seen from the hilltop basilica: an early lesson that the things worth building are the ones that endure.

As a kid I took apart anything within reach: radios, old computers, mechanical clocks. I needed to know what happened beneath the surface and never stopped until I did. At around twelve I wrote my first real program, and creating something from nothing turned out to be electrifying. The same instinct later pulled me toward cryptography.

At Árpád-házi Szent Erzsébet Gimnázium I balanced academics with fencing, which taught me to watch for the opening instead of swinging at everything, and to know when to commit and when to wait. Composure under pressure is still the skill I lean on most.

I read Computer Science at the University of Pécs, started building things that other people actually used, and found that the pleasure was less in writing code than in architecting a solution that makes a tangible difference. Today the fascination that began with a screwdriver is my profession: build something meaningful, build it well, never settle for good enough.

How I build

Four rules I keep

Assume someone is trying

A system is only as good as it is on its worst day, in front of its least friendly visitor. I would rather build for that day than be surprised by it.

Boring where it counts

Clever belongs in the problem, not in the plumbing. Postgres, a schema you could read out loud, and code the next person can follow without me in the room.

Take it apart

I do not really trust a library until I have read some of it. Half an hour in the source usually beats a day of guessing from the docs.

Finish the last ten percent

Error messages, logs, the README, the empty state. That last stretch is the whole difference between a demo and something people rely on.
